How to use a starter pack

Slot A comes from here

Choose one data source from the "Slot A options" in your pack. It should help answer SQ1: what is happening, and what human activity drives it?

Reject one

Your Slot A page needs one rejected source with a reason. The other Slot A option in your pack is a good candidate.

Cite the original

Open the link and cite that page or report in MLA, not "the starter pack". Record the date you accessed it.

Use the rest later

The other sources can help with your justification (Tuesday), Slots B and C, or background. They are a start, not the whole dossier.

◆

Once you have your research question (Tuesday 3 November), your issue is locked in. You can sharpen the wording, but you can't switch to a different pack. Every slot you fill after that must answer your question.

Example search trailI looked at both Slot A options in the Sahel starter pack. I chose the rainfall chart because it shows change over 40 years, which answers SQ1. I then searched "Sahel rainfall drought 1980s" to check the pattern appears in other sources.
Example rejected sourceI didn't use the IPCC biome diagram for Slot A because it is a projection for the whole of Africa, not measured change in the Sahel. It could be useful background for my justification.

A good rejection names the problem: wrong place · wrong time · not about people · can't tell who made it · repeats another slot.

4

Cite it in MLA

Organisation or author. "Title of the chart or page." Website name, day Month year published, URL. Accessed day Month year.

No author? Start with the organisation, e.g. NASA. No date? Leave out the published date, but always give the date you accessed it.

A chart inside a report? Cite the report and add the figure or page number. Never cite "the starter pack": open the link and cite the original.

5

Annotate by hand

on the printout, around the visual

Use Monday's four questions: What is it? What's the pattern? Why? What's missing? Arrows, circles and numbers from the chart are welcome.

Why it earns this slot

  • It was made by …, who … (why they know)

  • However, it does not show …

Starter pack 1 · Desert ↔ grassland transition

The Sahel

The Sahel is the belt of semi-dry land south of the Sahara, stretching across more than ten countries. It is a transition zone: too wet to be desert, too dry to be forest. Small changes in rain, or in how people farm and graze the land, can tip it one way or the other.

Starter pack 2 · Aquatic

Coral reefs

Coral reefs grow in warm, shallow, clear tropical water and hold about a quarter of all ocean species. When the sea gets too hot, corals push out the algae that feed them and turn white: bleaching. Hundreds of millions of people rely on reefs for food, jobs and coastal protection.

Starter pack 3 · Arctic / tundra

Glaciers & the Arctic

The Arctic is warming several times faster than the rest of the planet. Sea ice is shrinking, glaciers are losing mass, and tundra (the cold, treeless land with frozen ground beneath) is changing. That includes northern Norway, where Sámi reindeer herders depend on it.
